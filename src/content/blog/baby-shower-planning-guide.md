You've been asked to host a baby shower and suddenly you're staring at a blank screen wondering where to begin. Baby showers aren't taught in school. Between guest lists, registries, games, food, and the actual day-of logistics, it's easy to feel like you're juggling too much.

Here's the order to do things in — calm, simple, and actually fun.

## 1. Pick the Date and Venue (First, Everything Else)

Everything flows from these two decisions. Pick a date that works for the mother-to-be first — she's the one carrying a growing belly, attending appointments, and trying to stay comfortable.

For timing, aim for **six to eight weeks before the expected due date**. That's late enough in the pregnancy that she knows what she needs, and early enough that there's no last-minute panic if something comes up. If she's in her third trimester, **26 to 30 weeks** is a great window.

For the venue, think about what makes sense for the group size and the mom-to-be's comfort:

- **Your home** — most common for first baby showers. Set up in a living room or dining area. Cozy, no cost, and you control everything.
- **A park with a covered picnic area** — nice weather, plenty of space, but check weather backups.
- **A restaurant private room** — great for a larger group, eliminates your cooking workload.
- **A brunch spot** — baby showers and brunch are practically cousins. Low-pressure, daytime, food already handled.

Write the date and venue down immediately and mark it on the planning timeline. Everything else — invitations, shopping, food — is scheduled backward from this anchor.

## 2. Set Your Budget and Guest List Together

Baby showers cost money, and that's fine. But without a budget and guest list in place, costs can spiral in ways nobody planned for.

**Budget first.** Decide how much you're comfortable spending total — not per person, total. This determines venue, food, decorations, and whether you do anything fancy or keep it simple. Real 2026 numbers for a typical at-home baby shower:

- **Food and drinks:** $200–$400 for 20 guests (finger foods, cakes, drinks)
- **Decorations:** $50–$150 (balloons, banner, tablecloth, maybe a photo backdrop)
- **Invitations:** $0–$30 (digital is free; printed cards run about $1–$2 each)
- **Games and activities:** $0–$50 (printable games are free; some craft supplies if you're feeling ambitious)
- **Total range:** $250–$600 for a 20-person shower

**The guest list comes next.** This is the part most hosts find trickiest. You're not the parents-to-be, so the guest list should reflect *your* judgment of who belongs at this event. As a general rule:

1. Start with the mother-to-be's immediate family and closest friends — the people who will genuinely be there for her.
2. Add friends of the parents-to-be who you know well.
3. Don't feel obligated to invite coworkers, the parents-to-be's distant relatives, or people they only follow on social media. A baby shower is intimate by nature.
4. Keep the total between 15 and 30 people for a manageable, warm atmosphere.

Having the guest list helps you finalize food quantities and figure out whether your venue can comfortably fit everyone. A good guest-list tool — one where you can add people, track who's coming, and see dietary notes in one place — makes this significantly less stressful than a sticky-note pile on the fridge.

## 3. Build a Task Checklist with Real Deadlines

This is where most baby showers go off the rails. Not because the tasks are hard, but because everyone assumes "someone else will handle it" and then suddenly it's five days before the shower and nobody has bought napkins.

Here's a timeline you can copy and work backward from:

### 6 Weeks Before
- [ ] Confirm date, time, and venue
- [ ] Draft the guest list and send invitations
- [ ] Set your total budget

### 4 Weeks Before
- [ ] Follow up with guests who haven't responded
- [ ] Book any outside venue or restaurant if needed
- [ ] Start thinking about food — is it homemade, potluck, or ordered?

### 3 Weeks Before
- [ ] Confirm RSVPs with anyone still silent
- [ ] Order or buy decorations
- [ ] Plan the food menu and buy non-perishable items

### 2 Weeks Before
- [ ] Final headcount for food planning
- [ ] Confirm any potluck assignments with guests (who's bringing what)
- [ ] Buy perishable food items (keep them in the fridge)
- [ ] Prepare any games or activities

### 1 Week Before
- [ ] Buy remaining perishables — fridge space permitting
- [ ] Confirm with anyone bringing something
- [ ] Set up decorations at the venue (or prepare to set up the morning of)
- [ ] Make sure you have disposable plates, cups, napkins, and utensils

### Day Before
- [ ] Final grocery run for anything you missed
- [ ] Set up decorations and arrange the gift table
- [ ] Put perishables in the fridge or freezer

### Day Of
- [ ] Set up food and drinks 1–2 hours before guests arrive
- [ ] Put on some music (nothing too loud — this isn't a club)
- [ ] Breathe. It's going to be fine.

Notice the pattern here? The earlier tasks are strategic (date, venue, guest list, budget). The later tasks are tactical (buy napkins, put cake in the fridge). Each week, you do the thing that *enables* the next week's tasks. That's how baby showers don't fall apart.

## 4. Create a Wish List and Share It With Guests

Most people want to give something useful for a baby shower. Without a guide, they'll guess — and you might end up with three sets of matching onesies and zero packs of diapers.

A wish list solves this. It doesn't need to be a fancy online registry (though those work great). Write down **the things the parents-to-be actually need**:

- Diapers (newborn and size 1)
- Wipes (unscented, sensitive skin)
- Onesies/bodysuits in the next size up (0–3 months)
- Burp cloths and muslin swaddles
- Baby bath products (fragrance-free)
- Bottle sets (if formula feeding or combination feeding)
- High-chair or baby seat (higher-cost items)

Aim for **10–20 items** on the list. Not everything, just the essentials. Share this list with guests when you send the invitation — a text, a WhatsApp message, an email, whatever feels natural. The goal isn't to demand gifts; it's to help people give something the parents will actually use.

If you have the ability to export a formatted list as a PDF or share a document with multiple people, that's even better. Everyone can see what's available, and no two people accidentally buy the same thing.

## 5. Plan the Day-Of Timeline

A loose schedule helps you enjoy the party instead of running around putting out fires. Most baby showers last **2 to 3 hours** — long enough for food, gifts, and casual conversation, but not so long that guests get restless or the mom-to-be gets tired.

Here's a sample timeline for a Sunday afternoon shower (1:00 to 4:00 PM):

| Time | What Happens |
|------|-------------|
| 12:00 | You arrive early. Set up food, music, decorations. Put the gift table by the entrance. |
| 1:00 | Guests start arriving. Welcome, hand out drinks, let them mingle. |
| 1:30 | Food is served. Signal that it's time to eat (even casually — "Hey everyone, the food's on the table!"). |
| 2:15 | Gift opening. This is the main event. Seat the mom-to-be comfortably and go one gift at a time. |
| 2:45 | Optional: a quick game or two. Keep it light. |
| 3:15 | Group photo (if desired). |
| 3:30 | Wrap up. Thank guests. Help mom-to-be carry gifts to the car if needed. |
| 4:00 | Guests depart. You start cleaning up. |

The key insight: **food before gifts, gifts before games.** Nobody wants to eat while someone's opening presents, and nobody wants to play games when they're thinking about the food. The timeline above follows the natural energy flow of the party.

A good schedule or timeline tool — one where you can write out each part of the day with times and notes, and then export it to your phone or share with co-hosts — means you're never scrambling to remember what was supposed to happen next.

## 6. Prepare for the Unexpected

Some things will go wrong. A guest cancels last minute. The cake is the wrong flavor. Someone shows up with a dietary restriction you forgot about. None of this is catastrophic. Here's how to handle it:

**"Someone can't make it."** That's normal. It happens at every event. The food you planned for 20 guests will still work for 15. Don't stress.

**"The food isn't perfect."** People are there to celebrate, not critique. A store-bought cake that's "meh" beats a homemade one that's stressful to make. Lean on easy wins — fruit platters, cheese boards, store-bought dips with crackers.

**"A guest brings something awkward."** Maybe someone suggests a parenting technique the parents-to-be disagrees with. Smile, nod, change the subject. You're the host; you get to gently steer the conversation.

**"You're exhausted."** This is the big one. Host a baby shower and you *will* spend mental energy you didn't budget for. That's normal. The checklist from section 3 exists specifically to distribute the mental load so you don't carry it all on event day.

## 7. What to Do After the Shower

The party ends, the gifts are home, the house is slightly messy. Two things matter:

**Thank-you notes.** Not the host's job — the parents-to-be's. But you can help. If someone gives a gift but you don't know their name (maybe they came as a plus-one of an invitee), jot down a note somewhere so the parents can write a proper thank-you later. A simple index card with "Gift + name + giver's relationship" works.

**Send a group message.** "Thank you all so much for coming to Sarah's shower! She loved every gift and said the afternoon was exactly what she needed. Here's a photo from the day!" A single WhatsApp or text thread is warm, fast, and doesn't feel performative.

## What's Next

Planning a baby shower doesn't have to be a full-time job. If you break it down into the right order — date and venue, guest list and budget, a checklist with real deadlines, a wish list for guests, and a simple day-of schedule — you'll find it's more manageable than you expected.

The people who benefit most from this approach are those juggling baby showers alongside everything else: school pickups, work deadlines, family drama, the general chaos of being alive in 2026. When you put the guest list, RSVP tracking, a wish list, and a day-of timeline in one place instead of scattered across sticky notes, texts, and mental checklists, the whole thing suddenly feels like a party you can actually enjoy.
